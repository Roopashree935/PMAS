Privacy

Privacy Policy

How PMAS handles, stores, and protects patient data.

Summary: PMAS is an offline-first prototype. All data is stored only on the user's device in browser localStorage. No data is transmitted to any server. The research export removes all personal identifiers and uses Study ID + Study Day instead of raw dates.

1. Data Storage

PMAS stores all patient data — profile information, medications, symptom logs, appointments, adherence records, and consent — in the browser's localStorage on the user's own device. This data never leaves the device. There is no server, no database, and no cloud backend.

Important limitation: localStorage is convenient but is not encrypted storage. This is a prototype limitation. For actual patient deployment, stronger protection (such as device-level encryption or secure local databases) would be required.

2. Data Transmission

PMAS does not transmit any data to any server, API, or third-party service. The application functions entirely offline once loaded in the browser. There is no analytics tracking, no telemetry, and no background data collection.

3. Research Data Export

The research export feature produces a de-identified data payload that contains:

  • Study ID (e.g., PMAS-001) — assigned locally, not linked to patient identity in the export
  • Study Day (day 0 = baseline) instead of raw dates
  • Adherence records (taken/delayed/missed by study day and time slot)
  • Medication schedule patterns (morning/afternoon/night flags, duration) — no medication names or doses
  • Symptom scores (pain, temperature, weight, BP) by study day — no free-text notes
  • Appointment count only — no doctor names or notes

The export explicitly excludes: patient name, phone number, date of birth, emergency contact, doctor names, medication names, dose values, and all free-text notes.

4. Patient Health Summary

The Patient Health Summary is a separate, patient-owned report that contains full personal health information. It is generated on demand and displayed in the browser for printing or saving as PDF. It is never transmitted and is completely separate from the research export.

5. Consent

PMAS uses granular consent — six separate acknowledgements that must each be checked before the application can be used:

  • Understanding of the study purpose
  • Understanding of what information will be collected
  • Understanding that participation is voluntary
  • Understanding of the right to withdraw at any time
  • Understanding of how research data will be used
  • Understanding that the tool does not replace professional healthcare

The consent record (timestamp, version, language, and checkbox states) is stored locally on the device.

6. Right to Erasure

Users can delete all data stored on the device at any time using the "Delete All My Data" button in the Profile tab. This permanently removes profile, medications, symptoms, appointments, adherence records, and consent from the device.

Important distinction: Deleting local data does not automatically withdraw the user from the research study, nor does it erase data that has already been exported and stored separately by the research team. To formally withdraw from the study, the participant must contact their research supervisor.

7. DPDP Act 2023 Alignment

PMAS is designed in alignment with the principles of the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023, India. This includes local-only data storage, no server transmission, de-identified research export, granular consent, and right to erasure.

This is a design alignment, not a legal compliance certification. Formal compliance assessment requires review of the study protocol, consent process, retention policy, and institutional ethics committee approval.

8. Browser Notifications

PMAS can request browser notification permission for medication reminders. If granted, notifications are generated locally by the browser — no push server is used. Notification permission can be revoked at any time through browser settings.

9. Third-Party Services

PMAS does not use any third-party services, libraries, or APIs that transmit data externally. The application is entirely self-contained.

10. Limitations

This is a research prototype, not a licensed medical device. The privacy protections described here are appropriate for a prototype but would need to be substantially strengthened for clinical deployment, including:

  • Encrypted local storage
  • Secure data backup and recovery
  • Formal data protection impact assessment
  • Institutional ethics committee review and approval

11. Contact

For questions about privacy or data handling, please contact Kartik H through the contact page.
